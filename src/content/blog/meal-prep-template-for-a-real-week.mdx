Most meal-prep advice hands you five identical lunch boxes on Sunday and expects
you to still want them on Thursday. You will not. The version that actually
survives a week is not five finished meals — it is a small set of **components**
you cook once and recombine into meals that taste different each day.

This is the template I batch on a Sunday afternoon in about 90 minutes. It makes
roughly 8–10 servings across the week for one to two people.

## The template

You cook five things, in four groups:

- **A grain** — the neutral base
- **A protein** — the anchor
- **Two sauces** — where all the variety comes from
- **Quick veg** — cooked fresh in 5 minutes on the day, not batched

The sauces are the trick. Same rice and chicken tastes like three different
meals under a green herb sauce, a peanut-chilli sauce, or a squeeze of lemon and
yoghurt.

## Ingredients

**Grain (pick one)**

- 2 cups (400 g) rice, or 2 cups cooked quinoa, or 4 cups cooked pasta

**Protein (pick one or two)**

- 700 g chicken thighs, or 2 blocks firm tofu, or 3 cups cooked chickpeas

**Green sauce**

- 1 packed cup coriander + mint, 1 green chilli, 1 clove garlic, juice of 1
  lemon, 3 tbsp oil, salt — blend smooth

**Peanut-chilli sauce**

- 3 tbsp peanut butter, 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp chilli crisp, 1 tsp honey, warm
  water to loosen — whisk

**Quick veg (cook fresh daily)**

- Whatever is in the fridge: broccoli, beans, peppers, spinach

## Steps

1. **Start the grain first.** It is the most hands-off. Get rice going by the
   absorption method (rinse, 1:1.5 water, low heat, rest) while you prep
   everything else.
2. **Roast the protein in one tray.** Toss chicken thighs or cubed tofu with
   oil, salt and any dry spice you like. Roast at 220°C / 425°F for 22–25
   minutes (chicken) or 25 minutes (tofu, flipping once). One tray, no babysitting.
3. **Blend both sauces while the oven works.** Two minutes each. Store in small
   jars — they keep 4–5 days and are the reason the week does not get boring.
4. **Cool completely before you box up.** Warm food in a sealed container steams
   itself soggy and spoils faster. Spread the grain and protein on a plate for
   10 minutes first.
5. **Portion the components separately.** Grain and protein go in boxes; sauces
   and fresh veg stay apart until the day. Assembling at the last minute is what
   keeps every meal tasting made, not reheated.

## What actually matters

**Store components, assemble meals.** A fully-dressed salad is soup by Wednesday;
the same greens, grain and sauce kept apart are crisp all week. Anything with
crunch, freshness or sauce goes on at serving time, never in the Sunday box.

The second rule is **cool before you seal.** Condensation is the enemy — it
waters down flavour and shortens fridge life. Ten minutes of patience on Sunday
buys you three extra good days.

## A week from one prep

- **Monday:** grain + protein + green sauce, fresh spinach wilted in
- **Wednesday:** same protein, warmed, in a wrap with peanut-chilli sauce
- **Friday:** grain fried in a hot pan with an egg and whatever veg is left,
  green sauce spooned over

Same five things. Three meals that do not feel like leftovers. Scale the
quantities to your week and keep the two-sauce habit — it is the part that makes
meal prep something you keep doing.
