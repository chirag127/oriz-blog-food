Rice is the recipe people quietly give up on. It comes out sticky, or crunchy in
the middle, or fused into one sad brick. None of that is bad luck — it is almost
always too much water, no rinse, or lifting the lid to check. Here is the
absorption method that gets long, separate, fragrant basmati grains on the first
try, on a normal stove, no fancy kit.

Serves 4 as a side. About 25 minutes, mostly waiting.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup (200 g) aged basmati rice
- 1.5 cups (360 ml) water
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 1 tsp ghee or oil (optional, keeps grains glossy and separate)
- 1 bay leaf or 2 green cardamom pods (optional, for pulao-style aroma)

## Steps

1. **Rinse until the water runs clear.** Put the rice in a bowl, cover with
   water, swish with your hand, and pour off the cloudy water. Repeat 3–4 times.
   This washes away surface starch — the thing that makes rice gluey. This step
   is non-negotiable.
2. **Soak for 15 minutes.** Cover the rinsed rice with fresh water and leave it.
   Soaking lets the grains hydrate evenly, so they cook through without the
   outside turning to mush. Drain well.
3. **Get the ratio right.** For aged basmati, use **1 part rice to 1.5 parts
   water** by volume. Add the drained rice, measured water, salt, ghee and
   whole spices to a pot with a tight lid.
4. **Boil, then drop to the lowest heat.** Bring to a rolling boil uncovered.
   The moment most of the surface water has been absorbed and you see little
   craters forming (about 3–4 minutes), cover with the tight lid and turn the
   heat to its lowest setting.
5. **Cook 10 minutes. Do not lift the lid.** The steam trapped under the lid is
   what finishes the top layer. Peeking lets it out and gives you undercooked
   grains on top.
6. **Rest 5 minutes off the heat, still covered.** This is the step everyone
   skips. Resting lets the grains firm up and redistribute moisture. Then fluff
   gently with a fork — never a spoon, which crushes the grains.

## What actually matters

Two numbers do most of the work: **the rinse and the ratio.** Rinsing removes
the starch that causes clumping; the 1:1.5 ratio gives just enough water to
absorb fully with none left to make the bottom slushy.

The other half is **leaving the lid alone.** Rice finishes on trapped steam, and
every peek costs you a minute of cook and a layer of texture. Set a timer, walk
away, trust it.

## Adjust for your rice

- **Older basmati** (the good stuff, aged 1–2 years) drinks less water — stay at
  1:1.5 or even 1:1.4.
- **Newer or cheaper basmati** can take 1:1.75. If your rice always comes out
  crunchy, add a little more water; if it comes out wet, pull it back.
- **No tight lid?** Lay a clean kitchen towel under the lid to catch steam and
  seal the gap.

Once you have the ratio for your specific bag of rice dialled in, it never
changes. Write it on the container. That is the whole secret — rice is a
measured recipe, not a feel one.
