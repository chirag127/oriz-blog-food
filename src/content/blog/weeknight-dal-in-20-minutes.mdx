Most weeknight dal fails in one of two ways: it is watery and flat, or it took
an hour you did not have. This is the version I actually cook after work. The
lentils go in unsoaked, the whole thing is one pot plus a small tadka pan, and
the flavour comes from the tempering at the end — not from standing over the
stove.

Serves 4. About 20 minutes, most of it hands-off while the cooker does the work.

## Ingredients

**For the dal**

- 1 cup (200 g) toor dal (arhar / split pigeon peas), rinsed
- 3 cups (720 ml) water
- 1/2 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp salt, plus more to taste
- 1 medium tomato, chopped
- 1 green chilli, slit (optional)

**For the tadka (tempering)**

- 2 tbsp ghee or neutral oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 dried red chilli, broken
- 3 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 1/4 tsp asafoetida (hing)
- 1/2 tsp Kashmiri red chilli powder (for colour, not heat)
- A small handful of coriander, chopped

## Steps

1. **Cook the lentils.** Add the rinsed dal, water, turmeric, salt, tomato and
   green chilli to a pressure cooker. Cook on high for 4 whistles, then let the
   pressure drop naturally (about 5 minutes). No cooker? Simmer covered in a
   pan for 25–30 minutes until the lentils collapse, topping up water as needed.
2. **Whisk it smooth.** Open the cooker and whisk the dal for 20 seconds. You
   want it pourable but not thin — like a loose porridge. Add a splash of hot
   water if it has tightened up. Taste for salt now, while it is hot.
3. **Make the tadka.** Heat the ghee in a small pan over medium. Add the cumin;
   when it sizzles and darkens (about 20 seconds), add the dried chilli and
   garlic. Fry until the garlic is pale gold — do not let it brown, or it turns
   bitter.
4. **Kill the heat, then bloom the spices.** Off the flame, stir in the hing and
   Kashmiri chilli powder. The residual heat blooms them without burning. It
   should smell nutty and sharp.
5. **Pour and cover.** Tip the tadka over the dal and clap the lid on for 30
   seconds to trap the aroma. Stir through the coriander.

Serve with rice or roti. A squeeze of lemon at the table lifts the whole bowl.

## What actually matters

The single biggest upgrade is the **order of the tadka**: fat, then seeds, then
aromatics, and the powdered spices go in **off the heat**. Chilli powder burns
in about three seconds on a hot pan and takes the dal down with it. Cutting the
flame first is the difference between a dal that tastes deliberate and one that
tastes scorched.

If you only have masoor dal (red lentils), use it — it cooks even faster, in
about 12 minutes in a pan, but it breaks down softer and sweeter. Toor gives you
that slightly nutty, restaurant-style body.

## Make it your own

- **Creamier:** whisk in 2 tbsp of milk or a spoon of butter at the end.
- **Punchier:** add 1/2 tsp grated ginger to the tadka with the garlic.
- **Protein-heavier:** stir a handful of chopped spinach into the hot dal — it
  wilts in the residual heat in under a minute.

Leftovers thicken overnight in the fridge; loosen with hot water when you
reheat, and hit it with a fresh half-tadka if you want it to taste new again.
